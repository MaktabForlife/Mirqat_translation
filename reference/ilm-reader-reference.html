Al-Qārī’s introduction

Introduction · Mirqāt, vol. 1, p. 405

مقدمة كتاب العلم
ILM-I01 · IntroductionEnglish⌄

(كتاب العلم)

أي فضله وفضل تعلمه وتعليمه، وبيان ما هو علم شرعاً، وهو أعم من الكتاب والسنة، فيكون ذكره بعد باب الاعتصام من باب التعميم بعد التخصيص.

Scope of the book · new English draft

“The Book of Knowledge”: that is, its excellence, the excellence of learning and teaching it, and an explanation of what constitutes knowledge according to the religious law. Its scope is broader than the Book and the Sunnah; thus, placing it after the Chapter of Holding Fast [to the Book and the Sunnah] is an instance of generalisation after specification.

ILM-I02 · IntroductionEnglish⌄

والعلم نور في قلب المؤمن مقتبس من مصابيح مشكاة النبوة من الأقوال المحمدية والأفعال الأحمدية والأحوال المحمودية يهتدى به إلى الله وصفاته وأفعاله وأحكامه؛ فإن حصل بواسطة البشر فهو كسبي وإلا فهو العلم اللدني المنقسم إلى الوحي والإلهام والفراسة.

Acquired and divinely bestowed knowledge · new English draft

Knowledge is a light in the believer’s heart, kindled from the lamps in the niche of Prophethood—from the Muhammadan sayings, the Aḥmadan deeds, and the praiseworthy states. Through it one is guided to Allah, His attributes, His acts and His rulings. If it is obtained through human mediation, it is acquired knowledge, kasbī [كَسْبِيّ]; otherwise, it is divinely bestowed knowledge, ladunnī [لَدُنِّيّ], divided into revelation, inspiration and spiritual discernment.

ILM-I03 · IntroductionEnglish⌄

فالوحي لغة إشارة بسرعة، واصطلاحاً كلام إلهي يصل إلى القلب النبوي؛ فما أنزل صورته ومعناه ولا يكون إلا بواسطة جبريل فهو الكلام الإلهي، وما نزل معناه على الشارع فعبر عنه بكلامه فهو الحديث النبوي، وهذا قد يكون بغير واسطة في محل الشهود كما قال تعالى: ﴿فأوحى إلى عبده ما أوحى﴾ [النجم - ١٠] وقد يكون بواسطة نزول الملك، أي بنزوله من الصورة الملكية إلى الهيئة البشرية. وتحقيقه أن المتكلم الحقيقي هو الحق؛ فكلم أولاً محمداً بواسطة جبريل، وثانياً أصحابه بواسطة محمد، وثالثاً التابعين بواسطة الصحابة وهلم جرا. وقد يكون بنفثة في قلبه بأن يلقي معناه من غير أن يتمثل بصورة «إن روح القدس نفث في روعي».

Revelation and its transmission · new English draft

Waḥy [وَحْي], revelation, means a swift indication in ordinary language; technically, it is divine speech reaching the prophetic heart. That of which both form and meaning are sent down, and which comes only through Jibrīl, is divine speech. That whose meaning is sent down to the Lawgiver, who expresses it in his own speech, is Prophetic hadith.

This may occur without an intermediary at the station of witnessing, as the Most High says: “Then He revealed to His servant what He revealed” [al-Najm 53:10]. Or it may occur through the angel’s descent—that is, his descent from angelic form to human appearance. Its precise explanation is that the true Speaker is al-Ḥaqq [الْحَقّ], the Real: He spoke first to Muḥammad through Jibrīl, secondly to his Companions through Muḥammad, and thirdly to the Successors through the Companions, and so on.

It may also occur through a breathing into his heart, by casting the meaning into it without an appearance in a form: “The Holy Spirit breathed into my innermost heart.”

ILM-I04 · Introduction · †English⌄

والإلهام [لغة] الإبلاغ، وهو علم حق يقذفه الله من الغيب في قلوب عباده ﴿قل إن ربي يقذف بالحق﴾.

Inspiration · new English draft

Ilhām [إِلْهَام], inspiration, means conveying [in ordinary language]. It is true knowledge that Allah casts from the unseen into the hearts of His servants: “Say: My Lord casts forth the truth.”

The square brackets around [لغة] are present in the printed edition. Qurʾānic quotations in this draft follow the supplied scan; their independent reference and vocalisation check remains pending.

ILM-I05 · Introduction · †English⌄

والفراسة علم ينكشف من الغيب بسبب تفرس آثار الصور «اتقوا فراسة المؤمن فإنه ينظر بنور الله». فالفرق بين الإلهام والفراسة أنها كشف الأمور الغيبية بواسطة تفرس آثار الصور والإلهام كشفها بلا واسطة، والفرق بين الإلهام والوحي أنه تابع للوحي من غير عكس. ثم علم اليقين ما كان من طريق النظر والاستدلال، وعين اليقين ما كان بطريق الكشف والنوال، وحق اليقين ما كان بتحقيق الانفصال عن لوث الصلصال لورود رائد الوصال.

Discernment and the degrees of certainty · new English draft

Firāsah [فِرَاسَة], spiritual discernment, is knowledge of the unseen disclosed through discerning the indications of outward forms: “Beware the discernment of the believer, for he looks by the light of Allah.”

The distinction between inspiration and discernment is that the latter discloses unseen matters through discerning the indications of outward forms, whereas inspiration discloses them without such an intermediary. The distinction between inspiration and revelation is that inspiration follows revelation, not the reverse.

ʿIlm al-yaqīn [عِلْمُ الْيَقِين], knowledge of certainty, comes through reflection and inference; ʿayn al-yaqīn [عَيْنُ الْيَقِين], the eye of certainty, comes through unveiling and bestowal; and ḥaqq al-yaqīn [حَقُّ الْيَقِين], the truth of certainty, comes through realising separation from the taint of clay when the herald of union arrives.

Editorial note · not part of the source

The final sentence retains al-Qārī’s mystical imagery. The explanation is translated as his account, not substituted with a modern definition.

Transmitting knowledge faithfully

Hadith 198 · Mirqāt, vol. 1, pp. 406–408

الحديث ١٩٨
198-M · Hadith matn · Robson English · †English⌄

عن عبد الله بن عمرو رضي الله عنه، قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: «بلِّغوا عني ولو آية، وحدِّثوا عن بني إسرائيل ولا حرج، ومن كذب عليَّ متعمداً، فليتبوأ مقعده من النار». رواه البخاري.

James Robson · supplied English, unchanged

‘Abdallah b. ‘Amr reported God’s messenger as saying, “Pass on information from me, even if it is only a verse of the Qur’an; and relate traditions from the Bani Isra'il, for there is no restriction; but let him who deliberately lies against me come to his abode in hell.” Bukhari transmitted it.
Editorial note · not part of the source

Mirqāt includes رضي الله عنه with the narrator. The English is the supplied entry 1 without rewriting.

198-C01 · Mirqāt commentary · †English⌄

(الفصل الأول)

١٩٨ - (عن عبد الله بن عمرو) بالواو (قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: «بلغوا عني) أي انقلوا إلى الناس وأفيدوهم ما أمكنكم، أو ما استطعتم مما سمعتموه مني وما أخذتموه عني من قول أو فعل أو تقرير بواسطة أو بغير واسطة (ولو آية) أي ولو كان المبلغ آية، وهي في اللغة العلامة الظاهرة.

“Pass on information from me” · new English draft

[The First Section.] “From ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr”: ʿAmr [عَمْرٍو] is written with a wāw. “He said: God’s messenger said: ‘Pass on information from me’”: transmit to people, and benefit them, as far as you can or are able, with what you have heard from me and received from me—whether a saying, an act or a tacit approval, with or without an intermediary.

“Even if it is only a verse”: even if what is conveyed is one āyah [آيَة]. In ordinary language, an āyah is a manifest sign.

Editorial note · not part of the source

Quoted hadith expressions follow the supplied Robson wording where they match. Al-Qārī’s discussion of āyah extends beyond Robson’s interpretation “a verse of the Qur’an”; that distinction is retained.

198-C02 · Mirqāt commentaryEnglish⌄

قال زين العرب: وإنما قال آية لأنها أقل ما يفيد في باب التبليغ، ولم يقل حديثاً لأن ذلك يفهم بطريق الأولى لأن الآيات إذا كانت واجبة التبليغ مع انتشارها وكثرة حملتها لتواترها وتكفل الله [تعالى] بحفظها وصونها عن الضياع والتحريف لقوله تعالى: ﴿إنا نحن نزلنا الذكر وإنا له لحافظون﴾ [الحجر - ٩] فالحديث مع أنه لا شيء فيه مما ذكر أولى بالتبليغ، وإما لشدة اهتمامه عليه الصلاة والسلام بنقل الآيات لبقائها من سائر المعجزات ولمساس الحاجة إلى ضبطها ونقلها إذ لا بد من تواتر ألفاظها والآية ما وزعت السورة [عليها] ا ه. والثاني أظهر كما لا يخفى.

Why an āyah is mentioned · new English draft

Zayn al-ʿArab said: He said “an āyah” because it is the smallest amount that conveys benefit in transmission. He did not say “a hadith”, because that follows with greater reason: if verses must be conveyed despite their wide dissemination, the many who bear them because of their recurrent transmission, and Allah [Most High] having undertaken their preservation from loss and alteration—as He says, “Indeed, We sent down the Reminder, and We shall preserve it” [al-Ḥijr 15:9]—then hadith, which possesses none of the features just mentioned, is even more deserving of transmission.

Alternatively, it is because of his intense concern, blessings and peace be upon him, to transmit the verses: they remain when the other miracles have passed, and there is a pressing need to preserve and transmit them accurately, since their wording must be recurrently transmitted. An āyah is that [into which] a sūrah is divided. [End of quotation.]

The second explanation is more apparent, as is evident.

198-C03 · Mirqāt commentary · †English⌄

وقال المظهر: المراد بالآية الكلام المفيد نحو: من صمت نجا والدين النصيحة، أي بلغوا عني أحاديثي ولو كانت قليلة، فإن قيل: فلم قال ولو آية ولم يقل ولو حديثاً مع أنه المراد؟ قلنا: لوجهين أحدهما أنه أيضاً داخل في هذا الأمر لأنه عليه الصلاة والسلام مبلغهما، وثانيهما أن طباع المسلمين مائلة إلى قراءة القرآن وتعلمه وتعليمه ونشره ولأنه قد تكفل الله بحفظه. ا ه. والأظهر أن المراد الكلام المفيد وهو أعم من الآية والحديث؛ وإنما اختير لفظ الآية لشرفها، أو المراد من الآية الحكم الموحى إليه ﷺ وهو أعم من المتلوة وغيرها بحكم عموم الوحي الجلي والخفي، أو لأن كل ما صدر عن صدره فهو آية دالة على رسالته؛ فإن ظهور مثل هذه العلوم من الأمي معجزة والله أعلم.

Al-Muẓhir on meaningful speech · new English draft

Al-Muẓhir said: What is intended by āyah [آيَة] is meaningful speech, such as “Whoever keeps silent is saved” and “Religion is sincere counsel”: that is, pass on my hadiths from me, even if they are few. Should it be asked why he said “even an āyah”, rather than “even a hadith”, although that is intended, we answer in two ways. First, it too is included in this command, because he, blessings and peace be upon him, conveys both. Secondly, Muslims are naturally inclined to recite the Qurʾān, learn it, teach it and disseminate it, and Allah has undertaken its preservation. [End of quotation.]

The more apparent meaning is meaningful speech, which is broader than either a verse or a hadith. The word āyah was chosen because of its honour. Alternatively, āyah means a ruling revealed to him ﷺ, embracing recited and non-recited revelation because revelation includes both manifest and hidden forms. Or everything issuing from his breast is a sign of his Messengerhood, since the appearance of knowledge such as this from an unlettered man is a miracle. Allah knows best.

Editorial note · not part of the source

The scan reads مبلغهما, “conveys both”. The identity of al-Muẓhir is not expanded inside the translation; tracing this precise quotation in his own work remains a separate source-checking task.

198-C04 · Mirqāt commentary · †English⌄

قال الطيبي: وفي الحديث فوائد منها التحريض على نشر العلم، ومنها جواز تبليغ بعض الحديث كما هو عادة صاحب المصابيح والمشارق ولا بأس به إذ المقصود تبليغ لفظ الحديث مفيداً سواء كان تاماً أم لا.

Transmitting part of a hadith · new English draft

Al-Ṭībī said: The hadith contains benefits, including encouragement to disseminate knowledge and permission to transmit part of a hadith, as is the practice of the author of al-Maṣābīḥ and al-Mashāriq. There is no harm in it, since the aim is to transmit hadith wording that conveys a useful meaning, whether complete or not.

Editorial note · not part of the source

The source’s compressed attribution صاحب المصابيح والمشارق is retained rather than silently resolving the authorship of the two titles.

(وحدثوا عن بني إسرائيل ولا حرج) الحرج الضيق والإثم وهذا ليس على معنى إباحة الكذب عليهم بل دفع لتوهم الحرج في التحديث عنهم وإن لم يعلم صحته وإسناده لبعد الزمان كذا في شرح السنة، وتبعه زين العرب وأشار إليه المظهر، وهو مقيد بما إذا لم نر كذب ما قالوه علماً أو ظناً. قال السيد جمال الدين ووجه التوفيق بين النهي عن الاشتغال بما جاء عنهم وبين الترخيص المفهوم من هذا الحديث أن المراد بالتحدث ههنا التحدث بالقصص من الآيات العجيبة كحكاية عوج بن عنق، وقتل بني إسرائيل أنفسهم في توبتهم من عبادة العجل، وتفصيل القصص المذكورة في القرآن، لأن في ذلك عبرة وموعظة لأولي الألباب، وأن المراد بالنهي هناك النهي عن نقل أحكام كتبهم لأن جميع الشرائع والأديان منسوخة بشريعة نبينا ﷺ. ا ه.

Relating traditions from the Bani Israʾil · new English draft

“And relate traditions from the Bani Isra’il, for there is no restriction”: ḥaraj [حَرَج] means constriction and sin. This does not permit lying about them; it removes the supposition that there is sin in relating reports from them even when their authenticity and chain are unknown because of the remoteness of the time. This is stated in Sharḥ al-Sunnah; Zayn al-ʿArab follows it and al-Muẓhir indicates it. It is restricted to cases in which we neither know nor think that what they said is false.

Al-Sayyid Jamāl al-Dīn said: To reconcile the prohibition on occupying oneself with reports received from them with the permission understood from this hadith, what is intended here is recounting narratives of extraordinary signs—such as the account of ʿŪj ibn ʿAnaq, the Children of Israel killing themselves in repentance for worshipping the calf, and details of the narratives mentioned in the Qurʾān. These contain a lesson and admonition for people of understanding. The prohibition there concerns transmitting the rulings of their books, because all [earlier] religious laws and dispensations are abrogated by the religious law of our Prophet ﷺ. [End of quotation.]

لكن قال ابن قتيبة: وما روي عن عوج أنه رفع جبلاً قدر عسكر موسى عليه السلام وهم كانوا ثلثمائة ألف ليضعه عليهم، فنقره هدهد بمنقاره وثقبه ووقع في عنقه فكذب لا أصل له كذا نقله الأبهري. وروى الفقيه أبو الليث السمرقندي بإسناده في تنبيه الغافلين عن النبي ﷺ أنه قال: «حدثوا عن بني إسرائيل ولا حرج فإنه قد كانت فيهم أعاجيب» ثم أنشأ يحدث، أي رسول الله ﷺ فقال: «خرجت طائفة من بني إسرائيل حتى انتهوا إلى مقبرة، فقالوا لو صلينا ثم دعونا ربنا حتى يخرج الله لنا بعض الموتى فيخبرنا عن الموت ففعلوا ذلك. ثم دعوا ربهم. فبينماهم كذلك إذا رجل قد أطلع رأسه من قبره وهو أسود خلا شيباً، أي بياض رأسه يخالط سواده، وقال: «يا هؤلاء ما أردتم فوالله لقد مت منذ تسعين سنة فما ذهبت مرارة الموت مني حتى كأنه الآن فادعوا الله أن يعيدني كما كنت وكان بين عينيه أثر السجود؛

Two narratives and their attributions · new English draft

But Ibn Qutaybah said: The report that ʿŪj lifted a mountain as large as Mūsā’s army, peace be upon him, numbering three hundred thousand, to put it upon them, and that a hoopoe pecked and pierced it with its beak so that it fell around his neck, is a lie with no basis. Al-Abharī transmits this judgement.

The jurist Abū al-Layth al-Samarqandī relates with his chain in Tanbīh al-Ghāfilīn that the Prophet ﷺ said: “Relate traditions from the Children of Israel without restriction, for extraordinary things occurred among them.” He then began to relate—that is, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ—and said: “A group of the Children of Israel went out until they reached a graveyard. They said: ‘Suppose we pray, then ask our Lord until Allah brings out one of the dead for us, who can tell us about death.’ They did so, then called upon their Lord. While they were thus occupied, a man raised his head from his grave; he was dark except for grey hair”—that is, the whiteness of the hair of his head mingled with its blackness. He said: “What do you want, people? By Allah, I died ninety years ago, yet the bitterness of death has not left me; it is as though it were now. Ask Allah to restore me to the state I was in.” Between his eyes was the mark of prostration.

Editorial note · not part of the source

Quotation punctuation on the scanned page is irregular; it is not used to assign an independent authenticity judgement to the second story. The first judgement “a lie with no basis” is Ibn Qutaybah’s, transmitted by al-Abharī and quoted by al-Qārī. The earlier prose added Mūsā killing ʿŪj; that addition is not on this page and is excluded here. The reading أسود خلا شيباً is retained as printed; its exact pointing remains to be reviewed.

198-C07 · Mirqāt commentary · †English⌄

(ومن كذب علي) قال الكرماني: معنى كذب علي نسب الكلام كاذباً إليه سواء كان عليه أوله. ا ه. وبهذا يندفع زعم من جوز وضع الأحاديث للتحريض على العبادة كما وقع لبعض الصوفية الجهلة في وضع أحاديث في فضائل السور وفي الصلاة الليلية والنهارية وغيرهما، والأظهر أن تعديته بعلى لتضمين معنى الإفتراء. (متعمداً) نصب على الحال وليس حالاً مؤكدة لأن الكذب قد يكون من غير تعمد، وفيه تنبيه على عدم دخول النار فيه.

Deliberate fabrication · new English draft

“But let him who deliberately lies against me”: al-Kirmānī said that “lies against me” means attributing speech falsely to him, whether it is against him or in his favour. [End of quotation.]

This refutes the claim of those who allowed the fabrication of hadiths to encourage worship, as happened with some ignorant Sufis who fabricated reports on the virtues of sūrahs and on night and daytime prayers and other matters. More apparently, the verb takes ʿalā [عَلَى] because it incorporates the meaning of fabricating against someone.

“Deliberately”, mutaʿammidan [مُتَعَمِّدًا], is accusative as a circumstantial qualifier, not an emphatic circumstantial qualifier: a falsehood can occur without deliberateness. This indicates that the threatened entry into the Fire does not apply to that [unintentional case].

Editorial note · not part of the source

The printed words appear joined as عليه أوله. Here they are understood as عليه أو له, “against him or in his favour”, not “whether he or a predecessor originated it”, as stated in the earlier chat draft. This division and interpretation are provisional and explicitly recorded for review; the Arabic retains the printed spelling.

198-C08 · Mirqāt commentary · †English⌄

(فليتبوأ مقعده من النار) يقال: تبوأ الدار إذا اتخذها مسكناً، وهو أمر معناه الخبر يعني: فإن الله يبوئه. وتعبيره بصيغة الأمر للإهانة ولذا قيل: الأمر فيه للتهكم والتهديد إذ هو أبلغ في التغليظ والتشديد من أن يقال: كان مقعده في النار، ومن ثم كان ذلك كبيرة بل قال الشيخ أبو محمد الجويني: إنه كفر يعني لأنه يترتب عليه الاستخفاف بالشريعة.

An imperative of warning · new English draft

“Come to his abode in hell”: one says tabawwaʾa al-dār [تَبَوَّأَ الدَّارَ] when someone makes a house his dwelling. This is an imperative whose sense is declarative: Allah will assign him his dwelling. The imperative form conveys humiliation. It has therefore been said to express derision and threat, being stronger in severity and warning than “his abode will be in the Fire”. Thus this act is a major sin. Indeed, Shaykh Abū Muḥammad al-Juwaynī said it is unbelief, meaning that contempt for the religious law follows from it.

Ibn Ḥajar said: The suggestion in a commentator’s words that relating weak reports is prohibited without qualification is rejected. [End of quotation.]

It is known that most hadiths indicating subsidiary rulings are ḥasan. His own wording suggests this, since he did not say “through transmission of a sound chain”.

Nevertheless, it may suggest that the chain must be stated. That is not so: the meaning is that nothing should be related from him except what is established as coming from him. That establishment comes through transmission of the chain. Consequently, if something with a correct meaning is reported from him but has no chain, it may not be narrated [as coming] from him.

Envy and wishing for a similar blessing · new English draft

“And from Ibn Masʿūd, who said: God’s messenger said, ‘Two people only may be envied’”: envy has been defined as wishing for someone’s blessing to disappear and pass to oneself. In truth it is broader. It is blameworthy when acted on through determination, speech or action; hence the Most High’s words: “And from the evil of an envier when he envies” [al-Falaq 113:5]. An exception has been made where the blessing belongs to an unbeliever or an evildoer who uses it in disobedience to Allah.

What is intended here is ghibṭah [غِبْطَة], wishing to obtain a similar blessing oneself. The word envy is applied to it figuratively. Al-Ṭībī said: There is no permission for envy. Apparently the meaning is that, were envy permissible, it would be permissible only in these cases. The claim that the hadith permits a kind of envy because it contains religious benefit is not correct.

There is no contradiction between this restriction and his saying, blessings and peace be upon him, “Whoever establishes a good sunna in Islam has its reward and the reward of those who act upon it until the Day of Resurrection”, since the established sunna belongs to what is beneficial.

It has also been said that ribāṭ can be included in continuing charity, since its purpose is to support the Muslims. Deeds, lineage and spiritual rank · new English draft

“But he who is made slow”: the verb, with a doubled ṭāʾ, is from delaying, the opposite of hastening; slowness opposes speed. The bāʾ makes the verb transitive: someone whose bad action in the Hereafter—or neglect of righteous action in this world—delays him and slows his attainment of felicity “will not be speeded by his genealogy”. Lineage will not advance him or make up his deficiency merely because he has a distinguished ancestry among his people. Nearness to Allah is not obtained through lineage but through righteous actions, as the Most High says: “The most honoured of you with Allah is the most God-fearing of you” [al-Ḥujurāt 49:13].

Evidence of this is that most scholars of earlier and later generations have no lineages of which to boast; indeed, many early scholars were clients or freedmen. Nevertheless, they are the community’s leaders and fountains of mercy, while people of lofty lineage without those qualities, in their ignorance, are forgotten and disregarded. Hence his saying, blessings and peace be upon him: “Allah raises people through this religion and lowers others through it.” It is supported by the transmitted saying: “Ṣafiyyah, aunt of Muḥammad; Fāṭimah, daughter of Muḥammad: come to me on the Day of Resurrection with your actions, not your lineages, for I can avail you nothing against Allah.”

It is also related from Abū Yazīd—may [Allah] sanctify his secret—that a disciple followed in his footsteps behind him. He turned and said: “By Allah, by Allah, even if you flayed Abū Yazīd’s skin and wore it, you would not attain a mustard-seed’s weight of his stations unless you performed his actions.” He recited:

“Why are you content to soil your soul,

While your body’s garment is washed of dirt?

You hope for salvation without travelling its paths;

A ship does not sail upon dry land.”

“The man will reply, ‘I acquired and taught knowledge and read the Qur’an for Thy sake’”: I employed the blessing You bestowed on me in knowledge, action and recitation, seeking Your face and in gratitude for Your blessing.

(قال: كذبت) في دعوى مقام الإخلاص، أو على مقتضى عادتك (ولكنك تعلمت العلم ليقال: إنك عالم) ولعله لم يقل: وعلمت العلم ليقال: إنك معلم للاختصار والاكتفاء بالمقايسة، أو لأن أساس الشيء إذا لم يكن على الإخلاص فيبعد بناؤه أن يكون على وجه الاختصاص

The foundation of the undertaking · new English draft

“God will say, ‘You lie’”: in claiming the station of sincerity, or according to your habitual conduct. “You acquired knowledge that people might call you learned”: perhaps He did not add “and taught it so that you might be called a teacher” for brevity and because it follows by analogy. Or, if the foundation of an undertaking is not sincerity, it is unlikely that what is built upon it should be devoted exclusively [to Allah].

205-C09 · Mirqāt commentaryEnglish⌄

(وقرأت القرآن ليقال: هو قارئ، فقد قيل) لك عالم وقارئ فما لك عندنا أجر (ثم أمر به فسحب على وجهه حتى ألقي في النار) نعوذ بالله منها.

The reputation of a reciter · new English draft

“And you read the Qur’an that they might call you a reader, and they have done so”: you were called learned and a reciter, so you have no reward with Us. “Command will then be issued about him, and he will be dragged on his face and thrown into hell.” We seek refuge in Allah from it.

205-C10 · Mirqāt commentaryEnglish⌄

(ورجل وسع الله عليه) أي كثر ماله (وأعطاه) عطف بيان (من أصناف المال كله) كالنقود والمتاع والعقار والمواشي (فأتي به) على رؤوس الخلائق للافتضاح (فعرفه نعمه فعرفها قال تعالى: فما عملت فيها؟) أي في مقابلة النعم أو في الأموال

Every kind of property · new English draft

“Next a man whom God has made affluent”: He increased his wealth. “And whom He has given”—an explanatory conjunction—“all kinds of property”: coins, goods, real estate and livestock. He “will be brought forward”: before the assembled creation, to be exposed. After reminding him of His blessings and his acknowledging them, Allah asks what he did—in return for the blessings, or with the wealth.

205-C11 · Mirqāt commentaryEnglish⌄

(قال: ما تركت من سبيل) من زائدة تأكيداً لاستغراق النفي (تحب أن ينفق فيها) كبناء المساجد والمدارس وإعطاء الزكاة والصدقات (إلا أنفقت فيها لك قال: كذبت) أي في قولك [لك]

The claim of comprehensive generosity · new English draft

“The man will reply, ‘I have not neglected to give liberally for Thy sake to all the causes approved by Thee for this purpose’”: the additional min [مِنْ] emphasises the comprehensiveness of the negation. Causes in which He loves expenditure include building mosques and schools, giving zakāh and charities. “God will say, ‘You lie’”: in your words “[for Thy sake]”.

205-C12 · Mirqāt commentary · †English⌄

(ولكنك فعلت ليقال: هو جواد) أي سخي كريم (فقد قيل) وفيه إشارة إلى أن الله لا يضيع أجر من عمل لأي غرض يكون (ثم أمر به فسحب على وجهه) ثم هذا هو الأصل الصحيح من النسخ في هذا المحل وفي نسخة هنا أيضاً (حتى ألقي في النار، رواه مسلم).

The reputation of generosity · new English draft

“You did it that people might call you generous”: jawād [جَوَاد] means open-handed and liberal. “And they have done so”: this indicates that Allah does not let the recompense of someone who acted go to waste, whatever the aim of the action. “Command will then be issued about him, and he will be dragged on his face”: thumma [ثُمَّ], “then”, is the correct base reading of the copies at this point. Another copy has ḥattā [حَتَّى], “until”, here as well: “[until] he is thrown into hell. Muslim transmitted it.”

Editorial note · not part of the source

The matn on p. 419 reads ثم ألقي; al-Qārī records حتى as a variant. The commentary’s observation about recompense is translated as written, without adding an independent theological ruling.
