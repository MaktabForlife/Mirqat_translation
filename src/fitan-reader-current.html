Arabic first, English when needed

Tap or click anywhere in a tinted Arabic passage. Its translation opens inline immediately beneath the Arabic on every screen size. Tap again to close it. Hadith English is always visible directly below its Arabic matn. This is passage-level matching, not an automatic word-by-word dictionary.

Choose Study mode to display every translation below its Arabic passage. Use Search to find Arabic, English or a stable reference. The contents list moves between all 31 hadiths.

Use Enter or Space on an Arabic passage, Esc to close its translation, and the arrow keys while a passage or its translation has focus to move between passages. / opens Search. Arabic can still be selected and copied normally.

Arabic script beside transliteration

Transliterated words, phrases and grammatical terms being discussed in the commentary are accompanied by Arabic in square brackets. Personal names and book titles have not been exhaustively glossed.

This is the force of wa-innahu [وَإِنَّهُ] …

The brackets are editorial reading aids, not new words in al-Qārī’s Arabic. Where a dictionary or grammatical citation form is appropriate, it may differ from the surrounding inflection. Unsettled readings retain the review status of Draft 04. The original English remains stored separately; quoted Robson wording is not silently reworded.

Source and scope

5379–5409 inclusive. The three opening sections are included. Bāb al-Malāḥim, the chapter on battles, and hadith 5410 onwards are excluded.

Arabic base: Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī, Mirqāt al-Mafātīḥ: Sharḥ Mishkāt al-Maṣābīḥ, volume 10, edited by Shaykh Jamal Itani, Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyyah, Beirut, first edition, 1422 AH / 2001 CE. The project’s supplied Arabic scan is the main commentary source.

Hadith Arabic is the collated reading carried forward from Draft 04, checked in that draft against the Mirqāt scan, the supplied Sunnah.com Arabic and the Bushra edition. It is not a verbatim claim for a single witness. The hadith English is James Robson’s wording as supplied by the project editor, including its supplied notes and existing copy peculiarities.

The commentary English is a direct first draft from Mirqāt. Mazāhir-i-Haq was used selectively as a supplementary check, not as a replacement commentary. Historical, juristic and theological statements remain the source’s discourse, not new editorial rulings.

Review status

This interactive edition reuses the 31 hadith entries and 179 commentary units of Draft 04. It adds the reading interface and Arabic bracket aids; it does not claim a fresh scholarly verification of the entire text. Passage-specific review notes remain available under the English. The modern print editor’s footnotes are not all translated; they remain in the original source facsimile.

The landscape spread edition remains a separate, unchanged deliverable: its matn and English are uncoloured. In this online edition, hadith Arabic and English are also uncoloured; only commentary uses subtle tints for selection.

Local reading & privacy
